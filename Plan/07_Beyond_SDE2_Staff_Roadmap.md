Your stated plan: **SDE2 in 1 year → SDE3 in 2 years → Staff in 6–7 more months.**

Here is the truth, said with respect, because you asked for honesty:

| Transition | Your plan | Realistic (top 10% performer) | Why |
|------------|-----------|-------------------------------|-----|
| → SDE2 / L4 / E4 | 12 months | **5–10 months** ✅ Very achievable | You already have 5.3 YOE and strong design. This is a *hiring* problem, not a growth problem. |
| SDE2 → SDE3 / L5 / E5 | 2 years | **2–3.5 years** ⚠️ Aggressive but possible | Needs demonstrated *cross-team* impact over 2+ review cycles. Cannot be rushed by effort alone. |

**Total realistic arc: Staff by ~35–37, Principal by ~40–44.** That is an *elite* trajectory —
top few percent of engineers. The industry's fastest paths look like this, not faster.

### Why the correction matters (this is the important part)
Promotion above SDE3 is not gated on **skill**. It is gated on **evidence of scope over time**.
You cannot compress "led a 3-team, 18-month initiative that changed how the org builds X"
into 7 months, no matter how hard you work. Effort raises your *ceiling*; it does not
bypass the *calendar*.

**The good news:** what you CAN control is (a) never wasting a cycle, (b) picking high-leverage
teams, and (c) building promo evidence *deliberately* from day one instead of hoping. Below is
exactly how.

> Set the ambition at Staff. Set the *expectation* at "fastest possible, judged yearly."
> Chasing an impossible date is the fastest way to burn out at 30 and quit at 32.

## 2. THE LADDER — WHAT EACH LEVEL ACTUALLY MEANS

| Level | Scope | You are measured on | The promo question |
|-------|-------|---------------------|--------------------|
| **SDE2 / L4 / E4** | A feature or component | Delivering well-scoped work independently, good design judgment, reliable | "Can we hand them a project and stop worrying?" |
| **SDE3 / Senior / L5 / E5** | A system or service | Owning a system end-to-end, ambiguity resolution, mentoring, cross-team collaboration | "Do they make the *team* better and own outcomes, not tasks?" |
| **Staff / L6 / E6** | Multiple teams / a domain | Technical strategy, multi-quarter initiatives, unblocking orgs, influence without authority | "Would the org's technical direction be worse without them?" |
| **Senior Staff / L7 / Principal** | An org / business line | Company-level bets, industry-level judgment, growing other Staff engineers | "Do they change what the company is capable of?" |
| **Senior Principal / Distinguished** | Company / industry | Defining direction others follow for years | "Are they a reason the company wins?" |

**The pattern:** every level up, **the fraction of your impact that comes from your own keyboard
drops.** SDE2 = 90% your code. Staff = maybe 20% your code, 80% your influence, docs, reviews,
and judgment. Engineers who refuse to make that shift plateau permanently at SDE3.

## 3. YEAR 1 AT THE NEW COMPANY (the "land it" year)

**Goal: be ranked in the top 25% of your level within 2 review cycles.**

### Days 0–30 — Absorb
- [ ] Map the system: draw the architecture yourself, from reading code, not docs
- [ ] Meet 10 people 1:1 — ask "what's broken that nobody has time to fix?"
- [ ] Ship something small in week 1–2 (a bug fix, a doc fix). Momentum matters more than size.
- [ ] Learn the deploy pipeline, on-call process, and the review/promo calendar **immediately**
- [ ] Find out: who writes your promo packet, when, and what evidence it needs

### Days 30–90 — Contribute
- [ ] Own one meaningful component
- [ ] Take on-call and be genuinely good at it (fastest reputation builder that exists)
- [ ] Fix one thing everybody complains about but nobody owns → instant goodwill
- [ ] Write one design doc, even for a medium-sized change. **Writing is how you get seen.**

### Days 90–365 — Compound
- [ ] Own a project end-to-end: design doc → build → launch → metrics → retro
- [ ] Start a **brag document** (see §6). Update it weekly. Non-negotiable.
- [ ] Build 3 relationships outside your team
- [ ] Volunteer for the ambiguous, unowned problem. **Ambiguity is where promo evidence lives.**
- [ ] Explicitly ask your manager, in your first month: *"What does 'exceeds expectations' look
      like for me this year, concretely?"* — then do exactly that, and track it.

## 4. SDE2 → SDE3 (Years 1–3): OWN A SYSTEM

The one-line difference: **SDE2 completes projects. SDE3 owns outcomes.**

| Signal to build | How to build it deliberately |
|-----------------|------------------------------|
| **System ownership** | Become the undisputed owner of one important service. Know its metrics, cost, failure modes, roadmap. |
| **Ambiguity → clarity** | Take a vague business ask, produce a design doc that makes it concrete, get it approved. |
| **Cross-team work** | Lead one project that requires 2+ teams to change something. |
| **Mentorship** | Onboard a new hire, or mentor an SDE1 to a strong review. Get it in writing from them. |
| **Technical judgment** | Say no to something, with data, and be proven right. Document it. |
| **Operational excellence** | Own on-call quality: reduce pages, write runbooks, fix root causes not symptoms. |
| **Visibility** | Present at team/org tech talks. Write design docs others reference. |

### The promo mechanics nobody tells you
1. **Your manager writes your packet, but YOU write the evidence.** Give them a doc, quarterly.
2. **You need peer support.** Promo committees read peer feedback. Be generous, be helpful,
   review PRs well, unblock people. Those people write your promo feedback.
3. **You must be performing at the next level for ~2 cycles BEFORE you get promoted.** Start
   acting like an SDE3 in month 6, not month 24.
4. **Team choice is 50% of promo speed.** A growing team with headroom > a stable team with none.
   If your team has no SDE3 headroom, **transfer internally** — it's normal and fast.
5. **Never wait to be noticed.** It's not arrogance to make your work legible. It's the job.

## 5. SDE3 → STAFF (Years 3–7): SCOPE BEYOND YOUR TEAM

This is where most excellent engineers stop, because it requires a **change of kind, not degree**.

| Staff behaviour | What it looks like concretely |
|-----------------|-------------------------------|
| **Set technical direction** | Write the strategy doc for a domain: "here's where our data layer goes over 2 years" |
| **Multi-team initiatives** | Lead a migration/platform effort spanning 3+ teams for 2+ quarters |
| **Force multiplier** | Build the tool/framework/pattern 5 teams adopt. Your output = *their* velocity |
| **Glue work** | Do the unglamorous coordination that only works because a senior person does it |
| **Grow others** | Your mentees get promoted. That's the metric. |
| **Judgment at scale** | Called into the hardest decisions org-wide because your take is trusted |
| **Say no well** | Kill bad projects early, with reasoning that leadership accepts |

### The Staff archetypes (pick one — you can't be all four)
| Archetype | Fits you if… |
|-----------|--------------|
| **Tech Lead** | You like driving teams and delivery. *(Most common path from a strong SDE3.)* |
| **Architect** | You like owning a domain's technical direction deeply. **← likely your natural fit, given your HLD/LLD strength** |
| **Solver** | You like being dropped onto the hardest problem in the company |
| **Right Hand** | You like operating as leadership's technical arm across an org |

### Concrete Staff-prep actions (start these at SDE3, not later)
- [ ] Write one **strategy doc** per year that leadership actually references
- [ ] Own an **org-level metric** (latency, cost, developer velocity, reliability)
- [ ] Speak externally: a conference talk, a well-read engineering blog post
- [ ] Mentor 2–3 engineers to promotion
- [ ] Build a reputation *outside* your immediate org — sit on design review boards, interview loops,
      architecture councils
- [ ] Develop **business fluency**: know your product's revenue, cost structure, and competitors.
      Staff engineers argue in business terms, not just technical ones.

## 6. THE BRAG DOCUMENT (start it on day 1 of the new job — highest ROI habit here)

Weekly, 5 minutes, a running doc:

```
## <Quarter>
### Projects
- <Project>: what it was, my specific role, outcome + NUMBER, who benefited

### Impact beyond my team
- <Thing>: who I influenced, what changed

### Mentoring / reviews
- <Person/PR/design review>: what improved

### Ops / on-call
- <Incident>: what I fixed permanently

### Learning
- <Skill/domain acquired>

### Kudos received  (paste the actual Slack messages/emails — screenshots)
```

At promo time this becomes the packet in 2 hours instead of 2 weeks of trying to remember.
**Engineers who keep brag docs get promoted measurably faster.** It's not marketing; it's
solving your manager's information problem for them.
